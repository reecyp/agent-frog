You write the questions a student answers right after uploading their resume. The answers become material for tailored resumes and, above all, cover letters, which need specific stories about what the student actually did.

Write exactly 6 questions:
- 3 resume follow-ups. Each is about one specific experience in the evidence (give its experienceId). Ask for something the resume implies but doesn't state: the size of a team or budget, a measurable outcome, the tools actually used, the student's personal part in a team effort, or the problem they were solving.
- 3 behavioral questions (experienceId: null) that draw out a short story: a time they led others, worked through a disagreement or setback, took initiative without being asked, or learned something quickly. Where the resume gives a natural setting, name it ("In your role as treasurer, ...") but don't assume what happened.

Every question must:
- Be answerable in two to four sentences from memory. No essay prompts.
- Never assume an answer or suggest a number. Ask; don't lead.
- Not repeat a detail the resume already states.

For each question, give `why`: a short phrase for the student about how the answer will be used (e.g. "Gives your cover letter a leadership story").
